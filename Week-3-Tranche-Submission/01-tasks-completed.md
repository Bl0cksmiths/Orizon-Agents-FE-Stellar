### Epic 4 — Dispute Window & Partial-Credit Refund, Deliverable D3 ([BLO-8](https://linear.app/bl0cksmiths/issue/BLO-8), 28 pts this week) — 5 of 5 stories Done

Epic 4 gives the buyer a bounded window in which to say the work was not what they paid for, a platform-funded credit when that complaint is upheld, and a reputation consequence for the agent — each of the three visible on-chain or refused for a named reason. ### Epic 4 hardening — story 4.07 (no Linear card) — [BE #75](https://github.com/Bl0cksmiths/Orizon-Agents-BE-Stellar/pull/75), [FE #76](https://github.com/Bl0cksmiths/Orizon-Agents-FE-Stellar/pull/76), both merged 2026-09-25

A hardening pass over everything 4.02–4.06 shipped. On the backend, six read-only audits drove the real service and had to **prove** each finding before it counted; five fix lanes then fixed them, each with a test that fails without the fix and was teeth-checked by reverting. On the frontend, two audits proved 26 findings of their own against the real components and the real hook — engineering's internal count, separate from QA's D-numbered record below — and two fix lanes fixed every one. Backend diff +5,180 / −514 across 43 files, 113 commits; frontend +2,281 / −209 across 16 files, 39 commits.

**The money path**

- **A settler could pay a dispute twice.** `append_status` was an unconditional append, so two adjudications of one dispute both passed the status check and both signed. Reproduced: two transfers, 0.10 USDC out, and a record saying 0.05. `open → upheld` is now a compare-and-set whose precondition is a clause of the INSERT itself; a caller that finds the dispute already moved is answered with the existing credit, or refused `adjudication_in_progress`.
- **A stale decision could strand a live payout's mutex.** A data-modifying CTE runs whether or not the INSERT beside it writes a row, so the `refund_claims` DELETE fired for transitions the precondition had just refused. The mutex now moves only when the trail does.
- **A missing signing key parked a dispute forever** in `crediting` with a claim held, telling the operator the transfer "MAY HAVE LANDED — do not retry". The settler configuration is now checked **before** the transition: nothing is claimed, the dispute is left as the buyer left it, and the answer is `refunds_not_configured`.
- **A lost update in the append path.** `FOR UPDATE` inside the reading CTE does not fix it — at READ COMMITTED a statement that waits on a row lock does not re-take its snapshot, so the waiter copies forward the row it read before waiting. The lock is now its own statement in the same transaction, taken on the dispute's opening row.
- **A confirmed rating could be recorded beside a later, unconfirmed hash** — a tick over a transaction that never landed. `rating_confirmed` is now monotonic in both stores.
- **NaN defeated both refund caps** and wedged the transfer at `usdc_to_i128`; non-finite amounts are refused ahead of the caps.

**Disclosure**

- **Every dispute was readable by an anonymous stranger.** `GET /api/tasks` is public, so task ids led to disputes, which returned the buyer's verbatim `reason` and the adjudicator's `rejection_reason` with no credential at all. Chain-public facts stay open so shared trace links keep working; the **free text** now needs a task token or the operator key.
- **Any stranger could deny every buyer their dispute window.** One shared 500-entry challenge table with an eviction fallback that evicted _live_ entries. Proven over HTTP through the bind scope: 510 anonymous mints evicted a buyer's live dispute challenge. The budget is now partitioned per purpose (150/150/200, the memory bound unchanged), expired entries are swept, and a live entry is never evicted — the new mint is refused `503 challenge_capacity_{purpose}`.
- **Boot failures printed secrets into the deploy log.** A pydantic `ValidationError` carries the settings input dict, truncated head-and-tail, so which secrets escaped depended on the ordering of environment keys. A failed boot now raises a clean error built from the validator message and the variable name, with no exception chain left for a reporting tool to walk.
- **Four routes reflected caller input into `error.message`**, two of them world-readable while `TASK_AUTH_REQUIRED` is off. The dispute id is now 128 bits, since it is in practice the sole bearer capability on a buyer's complaint.

**Reputation**

- **One agent used twice lost half its ratings.** Every step's automatic rating was submitted under the run's `job_id`, which the ledger's `Rated(agent_id, job_id)` guard rejects as a replay before it reads `kind` — the same collision 4.04 solved for dispute ratings, here found on the ordinary settlement path. Ratings now derive a per-step id in the shape of ADR 0009 D1 under a distinct tag; step 0 keeps the sealed job id, so nothing already on-chain changes meaning.
- **Both steps were graded on whichever output landed last** — `delivered` was keyed by agent while its siblings were keyed by index. All four run-loop maps are now keyed by plan-step index, including `undispatched`, which otherwise dropped the rating for a step that delivered and was charged for.
- **A charge that times out records no settlement**, so a buyer who may have been charged cannot dispute at all. It is now logged as an unconfirmed charge naming payer, amount and job id, and the docstrings that claimed a missing job id meant no money moved are corrected — they asserted a guarantee the code does not provide.

**The receipt stops stating what it cannot show** (frontend)

- `refundArtifact` documents that an upheld dispute not yet sent is **pending, and never a hash**. The receipt said otherwise in three places at once, so a buyer went looking on Stellar Expert for a transaction nobody had attempted. "The credit is being sent to your wallet" became "the credit has **not** been sent yet — the transfer to your wallet is queued, and there is no transaction to look up until the platform submits it"; the on-chain caption now follows the artifact's state instead of being a constant; the mark reads "No transaction on record" rather than "Being sent" with no hash.
- **The panel could lie about money, permanently.** "Nothing on this workflow was charged" was terminal: if the read that fires when the run seals answered `settlement: null`, nothing ever asked again and the buyer was told their paid workflow charged nothing until they reloaded. And **a single 404 on any re-read destroyed a live receipt** — the old-backend fallback ran on every read, so one Render blip during a `crediting` poll wiped the receipt with no explanation and the poll never re-armed. The fallback is now first-read only; an unsettled-but-sealed run is re-asked on a bounded backoff before the copy asserts anything.
- **The clock overstated the window.** The offset absorbed the whole round trip, so a 45 s cold-start read rendered the window open 15 s after the server had stopped accepting disputes. It is now measured from the request, erring shut.
- **A dispute from a different job was pinned onto this settlement's step**, showing a refund for money that was not refunded on this run — and removing that step's Dispute button. **One unrecognised row blanked the whole receipt** and then failed every 5 s forever; a row this build cannot read now costs that row alone.
- **Accessibility and consistency:** completing a dispute dropped keyboard focus to `<body>`; the submit button disabled itself mid-signature, leaving no focus position and no way out for the length of a wallet round trip (now `aria-disabled`); the refund confirming — the one moment the buyer is waiting for — was announced to nobody; the character counter announced on every keystroke for the last 25 characters (26 announcements measured, now 25/10/0 only); the receipt and the dialog printed **different percentages for the same policy**, 6.3% against 6.25%, and the receipt rounded the buyer's credit up.

**Named as not fixed, in the PR itself.** A third settlement state (`charge_confirmed`) so an unconfirmed charge still opens a window without asserting the money moved; `refund_confirmed` on the dispute contract, so the refund is held to the same "the chain vouched for it" rule as the rating; and `app/services/binding_store.py`, which carries the same unbounded pool timeouts and lifecycle races that were fixed here in `dispute_store.py`.

**What is proven.** The code is merged on `main` in both repos. The dispute path produces both of the on-chain artifacts SOW §6.1 asks for, and they were produced on testnet from one uphold through `POST /api/disputes/{id}/uphold` — read out of Rie's roll-up, and re-read from Horizon on 2026-09-26 with `successful: true`:

**What those two transactions are not.** They came from Rie's own drill: the real backend and the real trace page running on her machine, against **her own ReputationLedger** ([`CAFZBHEK…5CRP`](https://stellar.expert/explorer/testnet/contract/CAFZBHEKKBPFR5RG33MJIMOLGXCP46E6ZGYBYISA3BCMVLY2H3RA5CRP)) and a test asset called **UATUSD**. The asset is not USDC, the ledger is not the platform's, and the service is not the deployment. Her roll-up states it in those words: _"These prove the code path. They are **not** Deliverable 3."_ This document does not upgrade that.

**Why not on the deployment.** There are two independent reasons, and the first alone is enough.

**The primary blocker: no run can be disputed at all.** No charge lands, so `_record_settlement` returns early, no settlement row is ever written and no dispute window opens. Driven for real on 2026-09-24 (`6.03a-dispute-path.md` §2–§3): two paid testnet runs both delivered and were both rated on-chain, yet each came back with `charge_tx` and `proof_tx` **null**; `GET /api/tasks/{id}/disputes` answered `settlement: null` on a task reading `complete` with a non-zero `spent`; and a challenge raised by hand against that run's own job id was refused `404 unknown_job`. That is **D-050** ([BE #67](https://github.com/Bl0cksmiths/Orizon-Agents-BE-Stellar/issues/67), Critical, open) — the consequence of **D-039** ([SC #3](https://github.com/Bl0cksmiths/Orizon-Agents-Smart-Contract-Stellar/issues/3)), the escrow defect that stops `PaymentEscrow.charge` moving the payer's funds. Nothing in the product says so: it looks like a feature that exists.

**The second: nothing could be upheld even if a dispute existed.** Re-checked against `https://orizon-agents-be-stellar.onrender.com` on 2026-09-26 while assembling this bundle:

- `POST /api/disputes/{id}/uphold` answers **`503 dispute_refunds_disabled`** — D-051, deployment configuration rather than code.
- `/openapi.json` lists all six dispute routes but declares **no security scheme**, so the deployment still runs a build older than the hardening merge `08efeda` — which means it does not yet carry the fixes for D-053 or D-056.
- `/readiness` answers `ready`, and the signer and the ledger's scorer are the same account, so a dispute rating _could_ be written once both blockers clear.

**So: no dispute can be raised or upheld on the deployment today, and Deliverable 3 is not captured.** The ordered path to capturing it is §5 of `docs/uat/evidence/6.03-dispute-refund-rating.md`: deploy backend `08efeda` or later, confirm `MAX_REFUND_USDC` is unset or finite, confirm `DATABASE_URL` is still set, and — the gating item, without which none of the rest matters — fix the escrow (D-039) so a charge lands and a settlement is recorded; then one live session with refunds on and `API_KEY` set — a settled run, a dispute raised from the trace page, an uphold through the API, and a continuous recording, following `docs/uat/checklists/6.03f-phone-and-screen-reader.md`.

The deliverable itself is set out in [`03-deliverable-D3-dispute-refund.md`](./03-deliverable-D3-dispute-refund.md), and the money-path guarantees the hardening pass turns on in [`04-money-path-safety.md`](./04-money-path-safety.md).
